1. Hotel Booking Analysis — Strategic Revenue & Cancellation Analysis

📌 Executive Summary

  • Business problem: Identify cancellation patterns, high-risk customer profiles, and revenue optimization opportunities across two Portuguese hotels to reduce financial losses and improve profitability.
  • Dataset: 119,390 real hotel bookings from two Portuguese properties — a City Hotel in Lisbon and a Resort Hotel in the Algarve — covering 2015–2017, including cancellation status, rates, market segments, distribution channels, and customer characteristics.
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, Seaborn, Matplotlib, SQL, SQLAlchemy.
  • Key findings:
    • 37% of all bookings were canceled during the analyzed period, with a significant gap between the City Hotel (41.7%) and Resort Hotel (27.8%).
    • The highest-risk customer profile is a Transient guest booking through an Online TA more than 90 days in advance, without a deposit, and with a history of previous cancellations (94% recurrence).
    • 53.7% of cancellations occurred during high-demand months (August, July, May, June, and April), when ADR reached its highest annual level (€125.17 per night).
    • Online TA is the most valuable distribution segment (€13.7M in generated revenue) while simultaneously presenting a high cancellation risk.
  • Potential business impact:
    • Total revenue lost to cancellations during 2015–2017 reached €16,721,837, with the City Hotel accounting for 65.1% of the losses (€10.8M).
    • Reducing the City Hotel's cancellation rate from 41.7% to 30% could represent approximately €3.2M in recovered revenue through targeted commercial policies.
    • Identifying high-risk customer profiles enables the hotel to intervene at the time of booking, before a cancellation occurs.
  • Final recommendation: Implement targeted deposit policies for high-risk Transient bookings made more than 90 days in advance, activate retention strategies during peak season, and encourage direct bookings to reduce dependence on intermediaries.

Business Objective

Strategic data analysis focused on identifying cancellation patterns and revenue optimization opportunities across two Portuguese hotels. The analysis develops an actionable high-risk customer profile and quantifies the financial impact of cancellations to support data-driven commercial decisions.

Tools & Technologies

Python | Pandas | Seaborn | Matplotlib | SQL | SQLAlchemy

Analytical Processes

Data Cleaning | Data Transformation | Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) | Cancellation Analysis | Customer Risk Segmentation | Revenue Analysis (ADR, RevPAR) | SQL Queries on Relational Databases

Key Business Questions

  • Who cancels? Which customer type, market segment, and distribution channel present the highest cancellation risk?
  • When do cancellations occur? How does booking lead time affect cancellation rates?
  • What is the financial impact? How much revenue is lost to cancellations by hotel, season, and market segment?
  • Where is the revenue? Which segments and periods generate the highest value while also presenting the highest risk?

Methodology

  • Data preprocessing: Cleaned missing values, corrected data types, filtered ADR outliers including negative values and extreme observations such as €5,400 per night, and created derived variables such as season, lead-time segments, and revenue metrics.
  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Analyzed booking trends from 2015–2017 and examined cancellation patterns by customer type, market segment, distribution channel, and booking lead time. Recurring seasonal patterns were identified.
  • Cancellation analysis: Developed a high-risk customer profile by combining customer type, distribution channel, lead time, deposit type, and previous cancellation history. The analysis confirmed a direct relationship between longer booking lead times and higher cancellation rates.
  • Revenue analysis: Calculated ADR and RevPAR by hotel, season, and market segment. Quantified revenue lost to cancellations by hotel, segment, and month, identifying a concentration of losses during high-demand periods.
  • SQL analysis: Queried a relational database to answer key business questions and validate findings obtained through Python.
  • Conclusions and recommendations: Developed an executive summary highlighting the high-risk customer profile, estimated financial impact, and prioritized actions for hotel management.

Key Visualizations

Executive KPIs: overall cancellation rate, total revenue lost, and average ADR by hotel. Summary of the financial impact during 2015–2017.

Cancellation rate by hotel. The City Hotel (41.7%) shows a significant gap compared with the Resort Hotel (27.8%), making the urban property the primary focus for retention initiatives.

Relationship between booking lead time and cancellation rate. Bookings made more than 181 days in advance reached a 57% cancellation rate, confirming a direct relationship between booking lead time and cancellation risk.

Revenue lost to cancellations during 2015–2017. The City Hotel accounts for 65.1% of total losses (€10.8M), defining the priority for intervention by property.

Cancellation rate by distribution channel. Online travel intermediaries consistently show higher cancellation rates than direct channels, supporting the strategy of strengthening direct booking incentives.

Conclusions & Key Findings

  • The overall 37% cancellation rate represents €16.7M in lost revenue during the analyzed period, with the City Hotel accounting for 65.1% of the financial impact.
  • The highest-risk profile is clear and actionable: a Transient customer booking through Online TA more than 90 days in advance, without a deposit, and with previous cancellation history (94% recurrence).
  • The concentration of cancellations during peak season increases the financial impact because it coincides with the months with the highest ADR.
  • Online TA represents the most complex segment: it generates the highest revenue (€13.7M) while also producing significant cancellation losses, requiring targeted retention strategies rather than exclusion.

Recommendations

  • Implement mandatory non-refundable deposits for high-risk Transient bookings made more than 90 days in advance, applying the policy according to customer risk profile.
  • Activate proactive communication during peak season (July and August), including upgrade offers, exclusive benefits, and booking reminders to reduce cancellations during periods with the highest ADR.
  • Develop direct booking incentives to reduce intermediary dependence, increase control over the customer relationship, and potentially lower cancellation rates.
  • Implement a repeat-cancellation alert system to automatically identify customers with previous cancellations and apply stricter booking policies at the time of reservation.

2. Contact Center Operational Efficiency Analysis

📌 Executive Summary

  • Business problem: Identify operational inefficiencies and critical factors affecting contact center performance, with a focus on missed calls and outbound call effectiveness.
  • Dataset: Operational records of inbound and outbound calls, including call duration, missed calls, and performance metrics by operator.
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, Seaborn, statistical analysis, and Tableau.
  • Key findings:
    • Missed calls and low outbound call effectiveness were identified as the main drivers of operational inefficiency.
    • Outbound calls showed a significantly higher missed-call rate than inbound calls, confirmed through statistical hypothesis testing.
    • Nearly 50% of operators were classified as medium- or critical-risk based on the operational efficiency score.
  • Potential business impact:
    • Potential productivity losses of approximately $6K USD per month were estimated from operational waiting time associated with critical operators.
    • Reducing the missed-call rate from 37% to the operational target of 5% could recover more than 10,000 additional customer interactions.
    • The analysis enables management to prioritize interventions for critical operators, optimize outbound calling processes, and improve overall contact center capacity.
  • Final recommendation: Implement operational monitoring dashboards and optimize contact schedules and calling lists to reduce failed calls and improve productivity.

  • What actions could improve operational quality and efficiency?

Methodology

  • Data preprocessing: Cleaned, integrated, and standardized operational data, checking for inconsistencies, duplicates, and missing values.
  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Analyzed variable distributions, call types, and operator performance to identify operational patterns and inefficiencies.
  • Business analysis: Identified critical factors associated with missed calls and operational performance, translating findings into actionable insights.

Conclusions & Key Findings

Recommended Strategies

  • Implement operational monitoring dashboards to track missed calls, call duration, and operator performance.
  • Strengthen training and call-time management practices to improve productivity and operational quality.

3. A/B Testing – Evaluating an E-commerce Recommendation System

📌 Executive Summary

  • Business problem: Evaluate whether a new recommendation system improves the conversion rate of an online store compared with the existing system.
  • Dataset: User behavior data from an A/B experiment, including navigation events, conversions, and performance metrics for control (A) and test (B) groups.
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, Seaborn, SciPy, and Matplotlib.
  • Key findings:
    • The test group (B) did not show a statistically significant improvement in conversion compared with the control group (A).
    • Overall funnel conversion was slightly lower in group B, with a difference of approximately -1.1 percentage points compared with group A.
    • The experiment presented relevant methodological issues, including group imbalance, potential user contamination, and temporal bias.
  • Business impact:
    • The analysis prevented the potential implementation of a product change without statistical evidence of a real improvement.
    • Methodological risks were identified that could have led to incorrect conclusions about the performance of the new recommendation system.
    • The findings provided recommendations to improve the reliability of future experiments and data-driven product decisions.
  • Recommended next steps:
    • Repeat the experiment with balanced groups and controlled random assignment.
    • Increase the sample size to improve statistical power.
    • Include additional business metrics such as revenue per user, retention, and average order value.
    • Evaluate alternative hypotheses regarding user behavior at critical funnel stages.
  • Final recommendation: Do not implement the current change until a new experiment is conducted with improved experimental design and stronger bias controls.

Business Objective

Evaluate whether the newly implemented recommendation system generated a statistically significant improvement in the online store's conversion rate by comparing a control group with a test group.

Tools & Technologies

Python | Pandas | Seaborn | SciPy | Matplotlib

Analytical Processes

Data Cleaning | Data Transformation | Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) | Conversion Funnel Analysis | A/B Testing | Statistical Significance Testing

Key Business Questions

  • Does the change implemented in group B generate a significant improvement across the conversion funnel?
  • Should the online store implement the proposed recommendation system?
  • Does statistical testing confirm that the observed difference is significant?

Methodology

  • Data preprocessing: Cleaned, integrated, and standardized the experiment data, checking for inconsistencies, duplicates, missing values, and potential data quality issues.
  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Compared sample sizes, user-level event distributions, and daily activity. Defined the eligible population and identified potential anomalies before evaluating the experiment results.
  • Conversion funnel construction: Built the conversion funnel to compare user progression across the main stages of the customer journey. Group A consistently contained more users than group B, revealing an initial imbalance that needed to be considered when interpreting the results.
  • A/B test evaluation: Compared conversion rates between groups across the funnel. Group B showed a small improvement at the product page → product cart stage, but this advantage was not sustained through the final conversion stage.
  • Statistical significance testing: Applied statistical hypothesis testing to determine whether the observed difference in conversion performance between groups was statistically significant.

Conclusions & Key Findings

  • Overall, group B did not outperform group A across the conversion funnel. The test group showed only a limited improvement at an intermediate stage, followed by weaker performance at the final conversion stage.
  • The implemented change did not generate a statistically significant improvement in the overall funnel. The observed difference was approximately -1.1 percentage points.
  • Statistical testing confirmed that the observed difference was not significant (p-value = 0.3024).

Recommendations

  • Do not implement the current recommendation system based on the observed results.
  • Repeat the experiment using balanced groups, controlled random assignment, and stronger controls for potential sources of bias.
  • Increase the sample size and experiment duration where appropriate to improve statistical power and reliability.

4. Book Database Analysis — SQL & Relational Data Analysis

📌 Executive Summary

  • Business problem: Analyze a relational database of books, authors, publishers, users, ratings, and reviews to identify patterns that could support product and content strategy decisions.
  • Dataset: Relational database containing information about books, authors, publishers, user ratings, and written reviews.
  • Tools: SQL, SQLAlchemy, Python, Pandas, Seaborn, SciPy, and Matplotlib.
  • Key findings:
    • Book production is highly concentrated among a small number of publishers, particularly Penguin Books and Vintage.
    • User ratings are substantially more common than written reviews, indicating lower participation in content creation.
    • Highly rated books are not necessarily those with the highest number of reviews, highlighting the importance of considering both quality perception and user engagement.
    • Author-level analysis revealed differences in productivity, average ratings, and review volume across the catalog.
  • Business recommendations: Use user segmentation and personalized recommendations to improve content discovery, while introducing engagement mechanisms to encourage users to contribute more written reviews.
📖 README 📓 Notebook

Business Objective

Analyze a relational database containing books, publishers, authors, ratings, and user reviews to identify patterns in publishing activity, content engagement, and perceived book quality, supporting data-driven product recommendations.

Tools & Technologies

SQL | SQLAlchemy | Python | Pandas | Seaborn | SciPy | Matplotlib

Analytical Processes

Relational Database Exploration | SQL Querying | Table Joins | Aggregation | Filtering | Data Validation | Result Analysis | Business Insight Generation

Key Business Questions

  • Which publishers have the largest presence in the book catalog?
  • How do user ratings and written reviews vary across books and authors?
  • Is higher perceived book quality associated with greater user engagement?
  • Which authors combine strong ratings, productivity, and user engagement?
  • What product strategies could improve content discovery and user participation?

Methodology

  • Database exploration: Identified tables, primary keys, foreign keys, and relationships between authors, books, publishers, ratings, and reviews.
  • SQL query development: Built queries using aggregation functions such as COUNT and AVG, filtering with WHERE and HAVING, and relational joins including INNER JOIN to answer key business questions.
  • Data analysis: Examined publishing volume, review activity, average ratings, publisher concentration, and author-level performance to identify relevant patterns.
  • Business interpretation: Translated database-level results into insights related to user engagement, content discovery, publisher concentration, and perceived quality.
  • Recommendations: Proposed strategies to increase written reviews through engagement mechanisms and improve book discovery through filtering, segmentation, and personalized recommendations.

Key Visualizations

Distribution of written reviews per user. The analysis shows that users contribute significantly more ratings than written reviews, highlighting an opportunity to increase participation in user-generated content.

Relationship between average book rating and number of written reviews. Most highly rated books have relatively limited review activity, showing that perceived quality and user-generated content volume do not necessarily move together.

Author-level comparison combining publishing volume, average rating, and review activity. The visualization highlights differences between highly established authors and authors with lower catalog presence or engagement.

Conclusions & Key Findings

  • A small number of publishers, particularly Penguin Books and Vintage, account for a significant share of the book catalog, indicating a concentrated publishing structure.
  • Highly engaged users who have rated more than 50 books write an average of approximately 24 reviews, suggesting that written content requires a higher level of user commitment than simple ratings.
  • Book ratings show substantial variation across titles, reflecting differences in author, content, and individual reader preferences.
  • The number of reviews should be considered alongside average ratings when evaluating book popularity and perceived quality.

Recommendations

  • Encourage written reviews through gamification, recognition systems, badges, or other engagement mechanisms.
  • Improve book discovery through filtering and segmentation based on genre, style, popularity, ratings, and user preferences.
  • Use author- and book-level performance indicators to support personalized recommendation strategies and increase engagement with less-visible content.
